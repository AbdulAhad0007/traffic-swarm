# Research methodology: controller evaluation

This document defines how the Traffic Swarm Intelligence (TSI) controllers are evaluated: what is
compared, what is measured, how it is tested statistically, and what the results can and cannot
support. Results live in the generated report (`experiments/reports/<matrix>/report.md`); this
file contains **no result numbers**.

Code version this describes: `simulation/version.py` = `1.3.2-eval`.

---

## 1. Research question and claims policy

**Question.** In SUMO simulations of generated grid networks, does the swarm signal controller
(PSO + ACO with a shared blackboard) change delay, travel time, stops, queues and emissions
compared with a fixed-time controller and SUMO's gap-based actuated controller, under identical
demand?

**Claims policy.**
- Every number in a report is recomputed from the raw run files of that matrix. Nothing is copied
  from an earlier report, README or summary.
- A "significant" result means the matched (paired) difference is unlikely under the null
  hypothesis *for these simulated scenarios*. It is not proof of real-world benefit and does not
  establish causation outside the experimental setup.
- Statistical significance and practical significance are reported separately (section 6.4).
- Incomplete matrices are reported as incomplete; failed, cancelled and superseded attempts are
  listed, never dropped silently.

## 2. Experimental design

### 2.1 Matrix

Defined in `experiments/config/evaluation_matrix.json` (matrix name `eval-v1`). A **cell** is one
(stage, network, scenario, seed). In a cell, all of the stage's controllers run on the same
prepared scenario.

| Stage | Networks | Scenarios | Controllers | Cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| core | grid3x3, grid5x5, grid9x9 | normal, rush-hour, uneven, sudden-congestion, road-closure | fixed, actuated, swarm | 150 |
| ablation | grid5x5 | all five | swarm, swarm_no_blackboard, swarm_no_pheromone, swarm_prediction | 50 |
| emergency | grid5x5 (3 ambulances) | normal, rush-hour | fixed, fixed_priority, swarm, swarm_priority | 20 |
| mixed | grid5x5_india, osm_bengaluru_mg_road | all five | fixed, actuated, swarm | 100 |

- **Seeds:** 42–51 (10 seeds, explicit in the config).
- **Run cap:** 3600 simulated seconds. A run ends earlier when the network is empty. Runs that hit
  the cap are flagged `timed_out`.

### 2.2 Matching (identical inputs)

For each cell, `run_experiment.prepare` builds the following **once**:
- the network,
- the route file (demand seed = run seed),
- the closure file,
- the SUMO configuration.

Every controller of the cell then runs on these identical files with the same SUMO `--seed`. The
route file's SHA-256 is stored in `comparison.json → metadata`. Differences between controllers
within a cell are therefore not caused by different demand. Different seeds vary both the demand
(turning-flow destinations) and SUMO's stochastic driver behaviour.

### 2.3 Networks
- **gridNxN:** generated by `netconvert` from `simulation/config/networks.json`.
  - Every junction is a signalized 4-arm junction with fringe approach roads.
  - 150 m blocks, 1 lane per direction, 13.9 m/s.
  - Netconvert default static programs: 30 s green, 3 s yellow, 1 s all-red.
- **grid5x5_india:**
  - Left-hand traffic, 2 lanes, sidewalks and crossings.
  - An **assumed** Indian urban vehicle mix and the sublane model.
- **osm_bengaluru_mg_road:**
  - OpenStreetMap extract (ODbL).
  - Uncontrollable signal programs stay native, with a reason recorded.

### 2.4 Demand scenarios (generated and OSM networks)

Demand window: 0–900 s.
- Grids use straight flows of 240 veh/h plus a seeded turning flow of 60 veh/h per entry.
- Scenario profiles multiply these flows over fractions of the window.

| Scenario | Definition |
|---|---|
| normal | ×1.0 over the whole window |
| rush-hour | ×1.2 → ×2.0 → ×1.4 (thirds) |
| uneven | ×1.0; N/S entries ×1.8, E/W entries ×0.6 |
| sudden-congestion | ×1.0, then ×2.5 between 33 % and 50 % of the window, then ×1.0 |
| road-closure | ×1.0, plus a **real closure** of one edge from t = 180 s to the end (see 2.5) |

### 2.5 Road closure (actual closure, not a demand multiplier)

**Mechanism.** A SUMO `rerouter` with `closingReroute`. All other edges are triggers, and
vehicles get `device.rerouting`.

**Edge choice.** The most-used interior edge is selected, subject to these rules:
- It is not a fringe edge.
- It is not the first or last edge of any trip.
- Every legal movement *through* the edge (predecessor → closed edge → successor, following lane
  connections without U-turns) must have a detour that avoids it.
- This check is unit-tested in `simulation/test_closure_selection.py`.
- **Version note.** Up to v1.3.1 only node reachability was checked. That allowed closures at grid
  corners, where some approaching traffic had no legal detour.

**Vehicles already approaching at closure start.**
- SUMO's rerouter acts only when a vehicle enters a trigger edge. `closingReroute` also does not
  change the router's weights (verified on SUMO 1.27.1).
- Each such vehicle therefore gets a prohibitive personal travel time for the closed edge and is
  rerouted (`simulation/runtime.py`).
- Two groups are recorded as *committed* and may still pass:
  - vehicles already inside the junction;
  - vehicles that cannot stop before the stop line at their normal deceleration
    (remaining distance < v²/2b).
- **Validator rule.** A cell is rejected if any other vehicle enters the closed edge while it is
  closed (`closure_check.vehicles_entered_during_closure` must be 0 for every controller).

The legacy J1–J9 preset's "road-closure" is only a demand multiplier. It is **not** part of this
matrix.

## 3. Controllers

| Controller | Behaviour | Sensing |
|---|---|---|
| fixed | Netconvert static program, unchanged | none |
| actuated | Same phases, yellow, all-red and clearances. Greens are variable in [min_green 10 s, max_green 60 s], extended while gaps are < `max-gap` 3 s (`simulation/baseline/actuated_controller.py`). | SUMO induction loops, one per incoming lane, at `detector-gap` 2 s upstream |
| swarm | PSO + ACO per junction, decision every 5 s. Only the remaining duration of the current green can be changed. | exact halting counts and occupancy via TraCI (**ideal sensing**) |
| swarm_no_blackboard | Agents publish no observations. The downstream-saturation discount and the neighbour-pressure term are therefore absent; the pheromone map stays active. | as swarm |
| swarm_no_pheromone | Each optimizer call gets an empty pheromone map, and nothing is reinforced after applied decisions. The blackboard observations stay active. | as swarm |
| swarm_prediction | The optimizer's queue inputs are replaced by the online predictor's one-step forecasts, only while its rolling MAE is acceptable. Timing limits always come from the current state. | as swarm |
| *_priority | same controller plus ambulance green-corridor priority (simulation prototype) | TraCI next-TLS look-ahead |

**Safety bounds shared by all controllers** (enforced by the independent `SafetyGate`):
- min green 10 s;
- max green 60 s, measured from the start of the phase;
- max red 120 s (anti-starvation);
- yellow ≥ 3 s;
- pedestrian minimum green 15 s.

**Safety audit.** A per-run signal-safety audit reads the states SUMO actually displayed. A cell
with any violation is rejected.

**The gate is never disabled.** No ablation turns the gate off. Its behaviour is tested separately
in `simulation/test_signal_controller.py` and `simulation/test_adaptive_contracts.py`.

**Fairness caveat.** The swarm has ideal sensing and the actuated controller has detector sensing,
so the comparison is not sensor-equal. Reports state this.

**Genetic-algorithm weight tuning is not implemented.** It is therefore not ablated, and no claim
about it is made.

## 4. Metrics

All metrics are recomputed by `experiments/tools/analyze_evaluation.py` from the raw files of each
controller run:
- `tripinfo.xml`, written with `--tripinfo-output.write-unfinished`;
- `statistics.xml`;
- the per-step network time series.

Only the following are read from the run's own `*_summary.json`, because no other raw file holds
them:
- fuel and CO2 totals (summed per step via TraCI);
- decision and optimizer latencies;
- online prediction errors.

| Metric | Unit | Definition | Denominator |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mean_total_delay_all_s` (**primary delay**) | s/veh | timeLoss + departDelay | all non-emergency vehicles that departed, including unfinished ones (censored at the run end) |
| `mean_delay_s` | s/veh | tripinfo timeLoss | arrived non-emergency vehicles |
| `mean_travel_time_s` | s/veh | tripinfo duration | arrived non-emergency vehicles |
| `stops_per_vehicle` | stops/veh | tripinfo waitingCount (speed < 0.1 m/s episodes) | arrived non-emergency vehicles |
| `mean_queue_veh` | veh | network-wide halting count, every 1 s step | steps in the demand window 0–900 s (same window for every controller) |
| `max_queue_veh` | veh | maximum network-wide halting count | whole run |
| `halted_vehicle_hours` | veh·h | Σ halting × step / 3600 | whole run (censored at the cap) |
| `arrived_vehicles`, `arrived_share` | veh, fraction | throughput | loaded non-emergency vehicles |
| `unfinished_vehicles`, `teleports` | veh, count | simulation failure indicators | — |
| `fuel_g_per_vehicle`, `co2_g_per_vehicle` | g/veh | SUMO default emission model | vehicles observed (all inserted) |
| `decision_latency_ms` | ms/tick | wall time of one network-wide decision tick | swarm controllers |
| `optimizer_ms` | ms | mean wall time of one PSO + ACO call | swarm controllers |
| `sim_wall_time_s` | s | wall time of the run | — |
| `ambulance_travel_time_s`, `ambulance_delay_s` | s | tripinfo duration / timeLoss of ambulances | ambulances of the run |
| `prediction_online_mae`, `_rmse`, `prediction_fallback_share` | veh, fraction | online one-step-ahead per-phase queue prediction error | swarm_prediction |

**Consistent denominators.**
- Per-arrived-vehicle means are biased when controllers leave different numbers of vehicles
  unfinished.
- Each paired test therefore reports `arrived_mismatch_max_pct`. If it exceeds 1 %, the verdict
  of a per-arrived metric carries a CAUTION.
- `mean_total_delay_all_s` (same vehicle set for every controller of a cell) is the primary delay
  measure.
- Queue means use a fixed window. Averaging over each run's own length would favour controllers
  whose runs end later with near-empty networks.

**Validity limits.**
- Fuel and CO2 use one passenger-car emission class for every vehicle, so they are **not valid**
  for the mixed-traffic stage.
- Wall times depend on the machine and on the number of parallel workers. They are cost
  indicators, not benchmarks.

## 5. Execution and provenance

Commands:

```
python -m experiments.tools.evaluate_matrix run --stage core --workers 10   # staged, resumable
python -m experiments.tools.evaluate_matrix status
python -m experiments.tools.analyze_evaluation                              # report from raw data
```

**Staged execution.** Stages and subsets can be run separately:
- `--stage`, `--networks`, `--scenarios`, `--seeds`;
- `--retry-failed`;
- `--rerun`, after a fix to a code path.

**Cell status.** A cell counts as *completed* only if its artifacts pass
`validate_run_artifacts`. That validator checks:
- seeds;
- safety audit;
- closure check;
- every junction registered and decided.

**Ledger.** `ledger.jsonl` is append-only. Each attempt records:
- status;
- error;
- traceback;
- wall time;
- `tsi_version`.

Before a re-attempt, the old run directory is **moved** to `superseded/<UTC stamp>/` and is never
deleted. The report lists every attempt (`attempts.csv`).

**Run metadata.** Each run's metadata records:
- SUMO, traci, netconvert and Python versions;
- platform;
- network hash;
- route hash;
- controller settings.

**Version history of the matrix.**
- Core cells for normal, rush-hour, uneven and sudden-congestion were produced by `1.3.0-features`.
- The fixes made since then touch only the road-closure path (closure rerouting and edge
  selection), so those cells are unaffected.
- All road-closure cells and all non-core stages are produced by `1.3.2-eval`.
- The report lists the versions per stage.

## 6. Statistical analysis (`experiments/tools/stats.py`)

### 6.1 Descriptives
Per stage × network × scenario × controller × metric, over seeds:
- n;
- mean;
- SD (ddof = 1);
- t-based 95 % CI of the mean;
- median.

### 6.2 Paired comparisons
Pairs are matched by seed within a cell (identical demand). For seed i,
d_i = controller − reference. Only seeds where both runs completed are used, and the number of
pairs is reported.

The test is chosen as follows:
- n < 5 → no test (`insufficient_pairs`).
- All d_i = 0 → `identical`.
- Otherwise the assumption check is Shapiro–Wilk on d:
  - p ≥ 0.05 → **paired t-test**;
  - p < 0.05 → **Wilcoxon signed-rank**.
- The exact **sign-flip permutation test** of the mean difference is always reported as a
  distribution-free sensitivity check.

Note on the pre-test: Shapiro–Wilk has low power at n = 10, and choosing a test after a pre-test
is a pragmatic rule, not an exact procedure. This is why the permutation p-value is always
shown.

**Effect size.** Cohen's d_z = mean(d) / sd(d). Also reported: the t-based 95 % CI of the mean
difference and the relative change (mean d / reference mean).

### 6.3 Multiple comparisons
- Holm–Bonferroni within each stage. The family is every network × scenario × comparison ×
  metric tested in that stage.
- Stages are not jointly corrected.
- Highly correlated metrics (fuel and CO2) are both in the family, which makes the correction
  conservative.

### 6.4 Statistical vs practical significance
Verdicts:
- **"Significantly better / worse"** requires a Holm-adjusted p < 0.05.
- **"Practically relevant"** additionally requires |relative change| ≥ 5 % (the matrix
  `practical_threshold_pct`).
- A significant change below 5 % is labelled *small*.
- Non-significant results are reported as "no significant difference", **not** as "equal".

## 7. Threats to validity
- **Synthetic demand.** Generated flows and an assumed vehicle mix, not calibrated against counts.
  OSM geometry is real; its demand is not.
- **Baselines are not optimised.** Fixed-time and actuated use netconvert/SUMO defaults. A
  Webster-tuned fixed-time plan would be a stronger reference.
- **Sensing asymmetry.** The swarm has ideal TraCI sensing (section 3).
- **Sample size.** 10 seeds per cell gives limited power for small effects. Seeds vary demand and
  driver behaviour together.
- **Censoring.** Unfinished vehicles' delay and timed-out runs are censored at 3600 s.
- **Emissions.** A single default emission class (section 4).
- **Timing measurements.** Shared-machine parallel load affects wall-time and latency figures.
- **Scope.** Simulation only. Emergency priority is a prototype, not certified for real systems.
